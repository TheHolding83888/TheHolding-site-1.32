## Canonical Decision Experience Record

For material decisions intended to teach the system, strongest evidence should be captured before the outcome is known:

`decision / disposition`
→ `rationale`
→ `confidence`
→ `expected outcome`
→ `evaluation criterion`
→ `review horizon`
→ `counterevidence`
→ `invalidation condition`
→ `rejected/deferred alternative when material`
→ later `observed outcome`
→ separate `decision-quality review`
→ separate `outcome-quality review`
→ `lesson`

If no pre-outcome record existed, retrospective reconstruction may remain as historical context, but it must not invent prior confidence or be treated as equally strong calibration / Founder DNA evidence.

## Decision quality ≠ outcome quality

Permanent learning rule:
- a favorable outcome does not prove weak reasoning was good;
- an unfavorable outcome does not prove sound reasoning was bad;
- confidence must not rise merely because the system or owner got lucky;
- later review must distinguish reasoning quality given contemporaneous information from realized outcome quality.

This is especially important for capital and onchain decisions where market movement, external protocol behavior, or randomness can dominate realized results.
