Why do you call them "funds" and "holding"? What do these terms mean?

Short answer: They're capital allocations. We call them "funds" because that's simpler than saying "capital allocation buckets."

The technical truth: Substantia, Defitea, Monetra, Fructus, and Singul are allocations — portions of capital deployed in specific proportions: 50/20/15/10/5.

But we call them "funds" because:

  • They function like funds — each has its own assets, objectives, and mission
  • They're structured like funds — distinct allocation tiers with clear boundaries
  • It's easier to understand — "five funds" is clearer than "five algorithmic capital allocation layers"

And why "The Holding"?

Because that's what it is — a holding structure. Like a traditional holding company that manages multiple subsidiary entities, The Holding manages five distinct allocations under one umbrella.

Think of Berkshire Hathaway: it's a holding company that owns GEICO, Dairy Queen, and dozens of other businesses. Similarly, The Holding oversees Substantia (foundation), Defitea (income), Monetra (stable yield), Fructus (real-world assets), and Singul (venture).

Simple language version: We split capital into five buckets. Each bucket has a job. Together, they work as one system. We call the buckets "funds" and the whole structure "The Holding" because those words make sense to people.

Five Funds Explained

Why five funds?

Capital is structured across five funds, each with a distinct goal and risk level:

  • Substantia (50%) — Foundation. Bitcoin, Ethereum, gold, silver. Fundamental assets for long-term capital preservation. Low risk, built to persist through every cycle.
  • Defitea (20%) — Income. Vote-escrowed DeFi positions: Curve, Convex, Pendle, Aerodrome, and others. Monthly cash flow from the infrastructure layer, with long-term growth potential.
  • Monetra (15%) — Stable Yield. A permanent liquidity reserve earning stablecoin yield from lending markets and liquidity pools. Operates independently of market direction.
  • Fructus (10%) — Real-World Assets. Tokenized real estate, commodities, and treasury instruments. Income from the physical world, independent of crypto cycles.
  • Singul (5%) — Venture. Asymmetric positions in AI, storage, and metaverse sectors. High risk, potential for outsized returns.

No single fund can perform across all market conditions. Together they form a deliberate, balanced architecture designed for long-term wealth building.

Why 50/20/15/10/5 allocation specifically?

This is deliberate risk distribution engineered for balanced growth:

  • 50% Foundation (Substantia) — Protects capital with BTC, ETH, gold, silver. The bedrock that doesn't change.
  • 20% Income (Defitea) — Generates cash flow through vote-escrowed DeFi positions. Sustainable yield without excessive risk.
  • 15% Stable (Monetra) — Stablecoin yield that works regardless of market direction. The liquidity layer.
  • 10% Real-World Assets (Fructus) — Income from tokenized physical assets, independent of crypto cycles.
  • 5% Venture (Singul) — Asymmetric bets on emerging sectors. Small enough to survive total loss, large enough to matter if one hits big.

The ratio balances stability, income, real-world diversification, and asymmetric upside across a single coherent system.

How are fundamental assets distributed within Substantia?

Within the Substantia fund (50% of total capital), assets are split evenly:

  • 25% → Bitcoin
  • 25% → Ethereum
  • 25% → Gold (XAUT)
  • 25% → Silver

Equal weight across four fundamental asset classes — maximum diversification within the foundation layer.

Why does Singul (venture) include new tokens if you've been building since 2016?

The venture allocation is designed to adapt. While our foundation (50% in BTC/ETH/metals) remains stable for years, the venture portion (5%) is our experimental zone.

This is intentional: Stability in the core, agility at the edges. We actively track emerging sectors — AI agents, decentralized storage, metaverse — and rotate positions as the market evolves. Singul reflects current opportunities, not 2016 bets.

How often do you update each fund?
  • Substantia (50%) — Assets don't change. We follow a long-term DCA (Dollar Cost Averaging) strategy to accumulate the four fundamental assets: Bitcoin, Ethereum, gold, and silver.
  • Defitea (20%) — Positions are periodically acquired and locked for yield generation. No fixed schedule — purchases happen anywhere from once a week to once a quarter, depending on market conditions.
  • Monetra (15%) — Stablecoin capital is deployed into lending markets and liquidity pools, compounding continuously.
  • Fructus (10%) — Tokenized real-world asset positions are added as suitable instruments become available onchain.
  • Singul (5%) — No set timeline. When a promising project appears, the fund may acquire it — sometimes in a single purchase, sometimes through multiple DCA entries to manage risk.

Technical & Ownership

How is a personal company assembled?

Each company is built individually, turnkey. A unique ENS name is registered as the company's onchain address, real assets are selected and placed on its balance sheet based on the chosen strategy, and the positions begin generating cash flow through the underlying protocols.

The whole process is onchain and verifiable at every step.

How do I request my own company?

Companies are currently assembled individually. You can explore the growing collection at theholding.ai/companies and reach out via our channel to start the conversation about your own.

Which blockchain networks are supported?

Companies are built primarily on Ethereum mainnet and leading L2s such as Base and Optimism, where the underlying protocols live. The exact setup depends on the strategy chosen for each company.

Who manages the company after it's built?

You do. Once a company is assembled, it belongs entirely to its owner. The Holding doesn't touch it, rebalance it, or manage it on an ongoing basis. The company works autonomously through the underlying protocols, and every decision beyond that is yours.

Is this custodial or non-custodial?

100% self-custodied. Every company remains fully under its owner's control — its assets, its key, and its rules belong to the owner alone. The Holding never holds funds, never holds keys, and never has the ability to move anything. Not custody. Not a broker. Not a fund manager.
